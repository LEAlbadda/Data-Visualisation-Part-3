Dubai Real Estate: The Urban Livability & Valuation Matrix

An interactive, multi-view study of 51,730 Dubai Land Department property sales (2015–2026) across 163 communities in 9 sectors, linked to 2,674 Foursquare venues.

How we cleaned the data

All five visualizations use the same Dubai Land Department sales data and cleaning rules: Visualizations 1, 2 and 5 use the community-level summary, Visualization 3 uses yearly summaries of home sales, and Visualization 4 uses the individual sales.

Data issue / decisionWhat we didEffect on the data
Transactions cover different types Kept records labelled Sales for property price analysis. Mortgages and gifts were not treated as market sales. Raw file: 89,212 records, including 68,286 sales, 17,617 mortgages and 3,309 gifts.
Dates outside the project period Kept transactions from 1 January 2015 through the latest date in the supplied file (17 September 2026). 51,731 sales fall within the selected period before the price check.

Community matching Joined transaction summaries to the community and venue data using the community code, rather than matching on names. 163 communities with qualifying sales are represented; 2,674 venues are linked to those communities.

Visualization 1: Urban Livability & Valuation Dashboard

Does a higher price per m² come with more amenities nearby? Each circle is a community.

How to use it
1. Brush: drag a rectangle over the scatterplot. Circles inside stay coloured and the rest turn grey, and the two bar charts below show only the selected communities. Drag the rectangle to move it, drag its edges to resize it, and double-click the chart to clear it.
2. Minimum sales slider: shows only communities with more sales than the value you pick. It starts at 100, matching the Part 1 feedback about filtering transactions greater than 100.
3. Quadrant dropdown: shows one quadrant only. “All Quadrants” brings everything back.
4. Together: the slider and dropdown choose which communities are eligible, and the brush picks from those.
5. Tooltips: hover over a circle or bar for details. A missing rating reads “No rated venues”.

Encoding

Each circle represents a community. The x-axis shows venue count, the y-axis shows median sale price per m² on a logarithmic scale, circle size represents sales count, and colour represents the dominant property type.

Interaction

Brush the scatterplot to filter both bar charts. The minimum-sales slider and quadrant dropdown refine all three views. Hover over a mark to see community-level details.

What it helps us see

The dashboard compares property values with nearby amenities and lets users inspect amenity composition and transaction types for selected communities. In the default view (60 communities with more than 100 sales), there is essentially no relationship between venue count and median price (Spearman ρ ≈ −0.005).

Visualization 3: Market Trajectories, 2015–2026

How did Dubai's busiest communities move through the off-plan boom: did prices rise because of new off-plan launches, or in established resale markets?

How to use it
1. Play or drag the year slider: each community's path grows year by year; the large dot is where it is in the selected year.
2. Community buttons: turn paths on and off. Paths that are off stay as faint grey context.
3. Click a path, dot or button name to show that community's price compared with all of Dubai in the chart below.
4. Hover a dot for that year's sales, off-plan share and median price.
Year: 2026
Communities (click a name to focus it)

Homes only (flats and villas, 46,653 sales), so land plots do not distort price per m². A community-year with fewer than 20 sales is left out and drawn as a gap. Medians also move when the mix of projects sold changes, so a rise is not the same as existing homes gaining value. 2026 runs to — only.

Encoding

Connected scatterplot: x = share of a community's home sales that were off-plan, y = median price per m² (log), one dot per year joined in time order, dot area = sales that year. The black dashed path is all of Dubai. The linked bar chart shows the focused community's median as % above or below Dubai's each year, coloured by off-plan share (same blues as Visualization 2).

Interaction

A year slider with Play animates the paths over time. Buttons choose which paths are highlighted. Clicking a path or name focuses that community, updating the linked bar chart and the summary. Tooltips give each year's exact values.

What it helps us see

Dubai's home sales moved from 36.9% to 69.8% off-plan (2015→2025) while the median rose from AED 10,869 to 18,299/m². Wadi Al Safa 5 went from 7.4% to 82.7% off-plan and its price doubled. Marsa Dubai rose 83% with off-plan staying below half, and in Burj Khalifa the off-plan share fell from 56% to 33% while prices still rose 38%.

Visualization 4: Property Price Distribution Explorer

How widely do sale prices per m² vary within and between property types, and how do off-plan and existing sales differ?

How to use it
1. Year brush: drag across the yearly bar chart to keep only those years. Click an empty part of that chart (or press Reset) to select all years again.
2. Property type buttons and registration menu: choose which sales are included in the histogram and boxplots.
3. Bins slider: changes how many bins the histogram uses.
4. Tooltips: hover over a bar or a box for exact values.
Property type

Price axis is logarithmic (AED 500 to 200,000/m²). The — filtered sales outside that range are included in the medians and boxplots but are not drawn in the histogram. 2026 runs to — only.

Encoding

The yearly bars stack off-plan (orange) and existing (blue) sales. The histogram uses bar height for the number of sales in each price bin, coloured by property type. Each boxplot shows the median, the middle 50% of prices (box) and whiskers at 1.5 × IQR on the log scale. Dots are outliers. The histogram and boxplots share one log price axis.

Interaction

The year brush, type buttons and registration menu cross-filter the histogram and boxplots together. The bins slider changes how much the histogram aggregates. Tooltips give exact counts and quartiles.

What it helps us see

Flats have the highest median (AED 16,322/m²) and villas the lowest (AED 10,769/m²). Buildings vary the most (IQR AED 24,067/m²). Off-plan flats have a median 51% higher than existing flats (AED 18,412 vs 12,217/m²). Land and buildings have no off-plan sales.

Visualization 5: Community Profile Parallel Coordinates

Which communities share a similar profile across price, sales volume, off-plan reliance, housing mix, amenities and population density?

How to use it
1. Brush an axis: drag vertically along any axis to keep the communities in that range. Brush several axes to combine conditions. Click an axis outside its brush to clear it.
2. Minimum sales menu: chooses which communities are drawn (the default matches Visualization 1).
3. Hover a line for that community's values. The selected communities are listed under the chart.
Line colour = dominant housing type
Apartments / Units Villas / Townhouses Mixed Typology

Median price, sales and population density use log-type axes. Population density is the 2019 population divided by community area, so it predates many new developments; some high-sales communities had almost no recorded residents in 2019.

Encoding

Each line is a community and each vertical axis is one attribute. Where a line crosses an axis shows the community's value on it. Colour shows the dominant housing type, using the same colours as Visualization 1.

Interaction

Brushing one or more axes filters the communities, so only lines that pass every brush stay coloured. The minimum-sales menu reduces unreliable small samples. Hovering highlights one line and shows its values.

What it helps us see

For the 60 communities with more than 100 sales, price rises with off-plan share (ρ = 0.51) and flat share (ρ = 0.54) and falls with villa share (ρ = −0.62). Price is unrelated to venue count (ρ ≈ 0.00), while venues go with population density (ρ = 0.46).
