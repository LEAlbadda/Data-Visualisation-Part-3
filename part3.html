Dubai Real Estate: The Urban Livability & Valuation Matrix

An interactive dashboard that links 51,730 Dubai Land Department property sales (2015–2026) to 2,674 Foursquare venues in 163 Dubai communities.

How we cleaned the data

Both visualizations use the same Dubai Land Department sales dataset and community-level aggregation. We applied these checks before interpreting the results.

Visualization 1: Urban Livability & Valuation Dashboard

Does a higher price per m² come with more amenities nearby? Each circle is a community.

How to use it
1. Brush: drag a rectangle over the scatterplot. Circles inside stay coloured and the rest turn grey, and the two bar charts below show only the selected communities. Drag the rectangle to move it, drag its edges to resize it, and double-click the chart to clear it.
2. Minimum sales slider: shows only communities with more sales than the value you pick. It starts at 100, matching the Part 1 feedback about filtering transactions greater than 100.
3. Quadrant dropdown: shows one quadrant only. “All Quadrants” brings everything back.
4. Together: the slider and dropdown choose which communities are eligible, and the brush picks from those.
5. Tooltips: hover over a circle or bar for details. A missing rating reads “No rated venues”.

Encoding

Each circle represents a community. The x-axis shows venue count, the y-axis shows median sale price per m² on a logarithmic scale, circle size represents sales count, and colour represents the dominant property type.

Interaction

Brush the scatterplot to filter both bar charts. The minimum-sales slider and quadrant dropdown refine all three views. Hover over a mark to see community-level details.

What it helps us see

The dashboard compares property values with nearby amenities and lets users inspect amenity composition and transaction types for selected communities. In the default 50+ sales view, the relationship between venue count and median price is weak (Spearman correlation ≈ 0.04).

3. What the charts show

The dashboard has two parts, one above the other. The scatterplot on top is linked to the two bar charts below it.

  • Sub-Vis 1, the scatterplot (one circle per community):
    • X-axis: number of Foursquare venues in the community (0 to 105).
    • Y-axis: median sale price per m² in AED, on a log scale from 1,000 to 100,000. The log scale is used because prices are right-skewed: most communities sit below AED 30,000/m² and a few are far higher. The cheapest community shown at the 10-sales minimum is about AED 1,280/m².
    • Circle size: number of sales in the community, 2015 to 2026.
    • Circle colour: dominant housing type. Blue means apartments (65% or more of sales), green means villas and townhouses (45% or more), and orange means a mix.
    • Brush highlighting: circles inside the brush keep their colour (88% opacity). The rest turn grey (22% opacity).
    • Dashed lines: at 15 venues and AED 13,000/m². These are thresholds we chose, not medians. The medians of the 91 communities with 10 or more sales are 7 venues and AED 14,484/m². A community counts as "high" when it is at or above the line, and each corner of the chart is labelled with its rule.
  • Sub-Vis 2, two bar charts for the selected communities:
    • Left, amenity composition: total venues in each of five categories (Dining & Cafes, Retail & Shopping, Hospitality & Nightlife, Leisure & Wellness, Services & Community).
    • Right, sales by stage and property type: off-plan and existing sales add up to total sales. Flats and villas are a separate breakdown of the same sales and do not add up to the total, because land and other property types are left out.

4. Interaction

  • Brush: drag a rectangle over the scatterplot to select communities. The bar charts then show only those communities. Double-click the chart to clear it. The brush is defined once at the top level of the Vega-Lite spec and attached to the scatterplot only (views: ["scatter"]). The bar charts read it with {"filter": {"param": "brush"}}. The link goes one way, from the scatterplot to the bar charts.
  • Minimum sales slider: keeps communities with more sales than the chosen value in all three charts (range 100 to 500, default 100). The default therefore applies the "more than 100 transactions" filter requested in the Part 1 feedback.
  • Quadrant dropdown: shows one quadrant only, in all three charts.
  • Tooltips: hover over a circle for the community name and number, quadrant, median price, sales, venues, housing type, off-plan share, average venue rating and area. A missing rating reads "No rated venues".

The slider and dropdown decide which communities are eligible, and the brush then picks from those. All three apply at once.

5. Findings

Research question: does paying more for property in Dubai mean getting a better lifestyle through access to nearby amenities? In this data, mostly not. Among the 67 communities with 50 or more sales (the default view), the rank correlation between venue count and median price per m² is about 0.04, or about 0.16 with the 10-sales minimum. Price and recorded amenities are only weakly related. The figures below are for the default view.

1. Urban Lifestyle Hubs (price of AED 13,000/m² or more, 15+ venues)
16 communities, e.g. Business Bay (3,660 sales, 43 venues, AED 20,318/m²), Marsa Dubai / Marina (2,967 sales, 100 venues, AED 21,739/m²), Burj Khalifa / Downtown (1,754 sales, 100 venues, AED 25,485/m²).
Note: High prices go with many venues here. The median is AED 22,808/m² and about 40 venues. Dining & Cafes is 46% of venues, hospitality 18% and leisure 16%. Foursquare collection stops at 100 venues per community, so Marina and Downtown are understated.
2. Affordable Active Communities (below AED 13,000/m², 15+ venues)
8 communities, e.g. Jabal Ali First (1,987 sales, 49 venues, AED 11,748/m²), Al Thanyah Fifth (1,786 sales, 51 venues, AED 12,490/m²), Warsan First (1,205 sales, 46 venues, AED 7,005/m²).
Note: The smallest group. Median price is AED 9,207/m², about 40% of the hub median, with a similar number of venues. Only 32% of sales are off-plan.
3. Exclusive Privacy Enclaves (AED 13,000/m² or more, fewer than 15 venues)
26 communities, the largest group, e.g. Madinat Al Mataar (2,083 sales, 12 venues, AED 14,483/m²), Al Merkadh (1,768 sales, 1 venue, AED 18,932/m²), Hadaeq Sheikh Mohammed Bin Rashid (1,746 sales, 3 venues, AED 19,165/m²).
Note: The typical community here has 1 venue but a median price of AED 17,147/m². Most (18 of 26) are mainly apartments and about 67% of sales are off-plan. That points to newer developments whose shops and cafes may not be built or listed yet, more than to villa areas chosen for privacy. The data cannot tell these apart. Private facilities are also not in the Foursquare data.
4. Emerging Suburban Corridors (below AED 13,000/m², fewer than 15 venues)
17 communities, e.g. Al Barsha South Fourth / JVC (4,004 sales, 4 venues, AED 12,924/m²), Wadi Al Safa 5 (2,183 sales, 7 venues, AED 12,578/m²), Al Hebiah Fourth (1,406 sales, 12 venues, AED 10,536/m²).
Note: Many sales and few recorded venues. About 54% of sales are off-plan overall, which fits with housing being sold before commercial amenities arrive, though this data does not prove it.

Data and Method

Datasets

  • Property transactions (transactions.csv): the course's Dubai Land Department file. We kept records marked "Sales", registered 2015 to 2026, with a positive price per m². That leaves 51,730 sales in 163 communities. We did not re-check the raw row count of the file.
  • Venues (dubai_venues.csv): 2,955 Foursquare venues. 2,674 are inside the 163 communities with sales and are the ones used. The other 281 are in 63 communities with no qualifying sales. Venues are sorted into five groups with simple keyword rules, so some are misplaced (pubs, BBQ and sandwich places end up in Services & Community). Of the 2,674 venues: Dining & Cafes 50.8%, Retail & Shopping 13.4%, Hospitality & Nightlife 13.2%, Leisure & Wellness 12.8%, Services & Community 9.8%.
  • Boundaries (dubai.geojson): community names, areas and 2019 population for 226 communities.

Joining the data

The tables are joined on the community code (COMM_NUM, called comm_num in the transactions file). Of the 226 communities in the GeoJSON, 163 have at least one qualifying sale, and all 163 match a boundary record. 138 of them have at least one venue and 25 have none. prepare_data.py also checks that off-plan plus existing sales equal total sales, and that the five venue groups add up to the venue count, in every community.

Calculations and cleaning

  • Median price: we use the median price per m² instead of the mean so that a few extreme sales do not distort a community's figure.
  • Venue density: venues divided by community area in km².
  • Off-plan share: off-plan sales divided by total sales, times 100.
  • Small samples: the data-preparation reliability flag uses 10 sales as its minimum. The 72 communities with fewer than 10 sales (243 sales in total) stay in the data files, flagged insufficient_data, but are never drawn. Their medians are not reliable; for example AED 1, 2 and 11/m² come from 1 to 3 sales. We cannot tell from the aggregated data whether those values are entry errors or nominal transfers.
  • Missing ratings: a community's average rating uses only rated venues. It is null when there are none, which affects 28 communities (25 with no venues, 3 whose venues are all unrated). It is never stored as 0.
  • Quadrant lines: 15 venues and AED 13,000/m² are chosen thresholds, not medians.

Limitations

  • Venue cap: Foursquare collection stopped at 100 venues per community, and two communities (Marina and Downtown) reached it.
  • Price per m² mixes property types: land plots and built units are both priced per m². Communities with mostly land or industrial sales (Saih Shuaib 1 and 4, around AED 1,300 to 1,500/m²) are not comparable with apartment communities.
  • Private amenities: clubhouses, private pools and similar facilities in gated communities do not appear in Foursquare data, so venue counts understate what some residents have.
  • Association only: the charts show how price and recorded amenities relate, not why.
